# PhD Application Strategy

## Application Positioning

The application should present the user as an Industrial Design lecturer whose teaching, design psychology, ergonomics, smart product design, and interdisciplinary project experience have gradually led toward doctoral research in design for ageing, community services, and responsible digital technology.

## Tentative Research Direction

The current working direction is intergenerational digital community services. This direction should remain open and flexible rather than being described as a fixed project.

Possible research focus areas include:

- ageing and intergenerational communities;
- community-based wellbeing;
- participatory design and co-design;
- service design for inclusive communities;
- design anthropology and ethnographic inquiry;
- responsible AI and human-centred digital services;
- situated prototyping in community contexts.

## Application Narrative

The core narrative should connect:

1. previous teaching and interdisciplinary design practice;
2. interest in real social contexts and community needs;
3. growing concern with ageing, care, inclusion, and intergenerational relations;
4. interest in design research methods such as ethnography, co-design, and service system mapping;
5. motivation to develop a PhD project that contributes to inclusive and responsible community-oriented design.

## Supervisor Contact Strategy

When contacting supervisors:

- mention 1–2 specific research themes, papers, projects, or methodological interests;
- explain a natural connection between the user's background and the supervisor's work;
- avoid generic praise;
- avoid claiming that the PhD topic is already final;
- ask whether the supervisor may be open to discussing possible PhD supervision or research fit.

## Risk Control

Avoid overstating:

- publication record;
- certainty of the PhD topic;
- technical AI expertise;
- direct experience with older adults if evidence is limited;
- fit with a supervisor when the connection is weak.

## Priority Evaluation Criteria

When comparing universities or supervisors, evaluate:

- thematic fit;
- methodological fit;
- supervision possibility;
- institutional support for design research;
- connection to ageing, community, service design, social innovation, HCI, or responsible technology;
- whether the user's background can be naturally positioned within the supervisor's research area.
