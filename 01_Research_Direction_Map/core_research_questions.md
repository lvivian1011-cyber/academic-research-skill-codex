# Core Research Questions

## 1. Main Research Direction

The broad research direction is:

**How can design support intergenerational digital community services in ageing societies?**

This direction connects ageing, intergenerational interaction, community wellbeing, participatory design, service design, and responsible digital technologies.

## 2. Core Research Question: Version A

### Intergenerational Digital Community Services

How can digital community services be designed to support meaningful intergenerational interaction, social participation, and community wellbeing in ageing societies?

### Possible Sub-questions

1. How do older adults, younger generations, families, and community stakeholders currently experience intergenerational interaction in local community settings?
2. What barriers prevent older adults from participating in digital or community-based services?
3. How can participatory and co-design methods involve older adults and other stakeholders in developing intergenerational community services?
4. What kinds of digital or AI-supported service features may enhance social connection, mutual support, and local knowledge exchange?
5. How can designers ensure that digital community services remain inclusive, accessible, ethical, and socially meaningful?

## 3. Core Research Question: Version B

### Participatory Design and Ageing

How can participatory design and co-design methods support older adults' active involvement in the development of inclusive community-based services?

### Possible Sub-questions

1. What forms of participation are meaningful and appropriate for older adults in community service design?
2. How can co-design workshops be adapted to include older adults with different physical, cognitive, social, and digital abilities?
3. How do power relations between designers, researchers, institutions, families, and older adults affect participatory design processes?
4. What design tools can help older adults express needs, memories, emotions, routines, and aspirations?
5. How can participatory design move beyond consultation and become a process of shared service development?

## 4. Core Research Question: Version C

### Design Anthropology and Everyday Ageing

How can design anthropology help reveal older adults' everyday experiences of community, care, digital services, and intergenerational relations?

### Possible Sub-questions

1. How do older adults experience ageing, care, independence, and social participation in everyday community life?
2. What informal practices of mutual support already exist between older adults, families, neighbours, and younger generations?
3. How do local culture, place, memory, and routine shape older adults' engagement with community services?
4. How can ethnographic insights inform the design of more situated and meaningful digital community services?
5. How can design interventions respect older adults' lived experiences rather than imposing top-down technological solutions?

## 5. Core Research Question: Version D

### Responsible AI and Community Wellbeing

How can AI-supported community services be designed responsibly to enhance older adults' wellbeing, autonomy, and social connection?

### Possible Sub-questions

1. What opportunities and risks emerge when AI-supported tools are introduced into ageing-related community services?
2. How do older adults perceive AI-supported services in relation to trust, autonomy, privacy, care, and social connection?
3. How can responsible AI principles be translated into practical service design methods for community contexts?
4. How can AI-supported services avoid reinforcing digital exclusion or dependence?
5. What design framework can guide the responsible development of AI-supported intergenerational community services?

## 6. Core Research Question: Version E

### Shared Spaces and Intergenerational Community Care

How can shared community spaces and digital services be designed to support intergenerational learning, care, and mutual support?

### Possible Sub-questions

1. What types of shared spaces encourage interaction between older adults and younger generations?
2. How do physical, social, and digital environments influence intergenerational engagement?
3. How can service design connect offline community spaces with digital participation?
4. What roles can older adults play as knowledge holders, mentors, learners, carers, or community contributors?
5. How can intergenerational services contribute to broader community wellbeing?

## 7. Current Preferred Research Question

The current preferred version is:

**How can participatory and co-design methods support the development of inclusive digital community services that enhance older adults' social participation and intergenerational community wellbeing?**

This version is currently the most balanced because it connects:

- Older adults
- Intergenerational communities
- Digital community services
- Participatory design
- Co-design
- Social participation
- Community wellbeing

It is suitable for PhD applications in design research, service design, participatory design, design anthropology, ageing studies, and human-centred technology research.

## 8. Notes for Future Refinement

The research question should remain flexible at the early PhD application stage. It can be adjusted depending on the supervisor, institution, research group, and funding call.

Possible emphasis by supervisor type:

- Service design supervisor: focus on community service systems and stakeholder participation
- Design anthropology supervisor: focus on everyday ageing, ethnography, and situated practices
- HCI or digital technology supervisor: focus on digital inclusion, interaction, and responsible AI
- Ageing studies supervisor: focus on older adults' wellbeing, participation, and ageing in place
- Social design supervisor: focus on community innovation, care, and social impact
