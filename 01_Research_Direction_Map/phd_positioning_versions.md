# PhD Positioning Versions

## 1. Purpose of This File

This file collects different versions of my PhD research positioning. These versions can be adapted for different supervisors, universities, departments, research groups, motivation letters, personal statements, and research proposals.

The broad research direction is:

**Intergenerational digital community services for ageing societies.**

The positioning can shift depending on whether the supervisor is more focused on service design, participatory design, design anthropology, ageing studies, HCI, responsible AI, or social innovation.

## 2. Version 1: General PhD Positioning

My proposed PhD direction focuses on how participatory and service design approaches can support the development of inclusive digital community services for ageing societies. I am particularly interested in how older adults, younger generations, families, local organisations, and community stakeholders can be involved in co-designing services that promote social participation, intergenerational interaction, and community wellbeing.

Building on my background in industrial design, human factors, design psychology, and interdisciplinary design education, I aim to move from product-centred design toward community-based and socially engaged design research. My PhD research would explore how design methods can help create more inclusive, situated, and responsible service systems for older adults and intergenerational communities.

## 3. Version 2: Service Design Positioning

My PhD interest lies in service design for ageing and intergenerational community wellbeing. I am interested in how community-based services can be designed as interconnected systems involving older adults, younger generations, families, community organisations, public services, and digital platforms.

Rather than focusing only on a single product or interface, my research aims to investigate how service design methods, such as stakeholder mapping, user journey mapping, service blueprinting, and co-design workshops, can support the development of inclusive and sustainable community service systems.

This positioning is especially suitable for supervisors working on:

- Service design
- Social innovation
- Public service design
- Community-based design
- Product-service systems
- Design for wellbeing

## 4. Version 3: Participatory Design and Co-design Positioning

My proposed PhD research focuses on how participatory design and co-design methods can involve older adults and community stakeholders in developing inclusive intergenerational community services.

I am interested in older adults not only as service users, but also as active contributors who hold local knowledge, life experience, skills, memories, and social connections. Through participatory workshops, ethnographic inquiry, and situated prototyping, my research would explore how design can support more democratic and meaningful forms of participation in ageing-related community service development.

This positioning is especially suitable for supervisors working on:

- Participatory design
- Co-design
- Design for social innovation
- Community engagement
- Democratic design
- Design methods

## 5. Version 4: Design Anthropology Positioning

My research interest lies in understanding ageing, care, community, and digital services through design anthropology and ethnographic design research. I am interested in how older adults experience everyday community life, how intergenerational relations are formed or weakened, and how digital services are embedded in social practices, places, routines, and local cultures.

Rather than beginning with a predefined technological solution, I hope to use ethnographic and participatory design approaches to understand the lived experiences of older adults and community stakeholders. This could inform the development of more situated, inclusive, and culturally meaningful community services.

This positioning is especially suitable for supervisors working on:

- Design anthropology
- Ethnography
- Everyday life
- Social design
- Community-based research
- Human-centred design

## 6. Version 5: Responsible AI and Digital Community Positioning

My PhD direction explores how responsible AI and digital technologies can be integrated into community-based services for ageing societies. I am particularly interested in how AI-supported services can enhance older adults' social participation, autonomy, access to community resources, and intergenerational connection while avoiding digital exclusion, privacy risks, and top-down technological implementation.

The research would combine participatory design, service design, and responsible AI perspectives to investigate how older adults and community stakeholders can be meaningfully involved in shaping AI-supported community services.

This positioning is especially suitable for supervisors working on:

- Human-centred AI
- Responsible AI
- HCI
- Digital inclusion
- AI-supported services
- Sociotechnical systems
- Technology and ageing

The research would examine how design can contribute to age-friendly communities by supporting everyday participation, mutual support, local knowledge exchange, and inclusive service experiences.

This positioning is especially suitable for supervisors working on:

- Age-friendly communities

- Social participation
- Care and wellbeing

## 15. Research Proposal Version

This proposed PhD research investigates how participatory and service design approaches can support the development of inclusive intergenerational digital community services in ageing societies. The research responds to the growing need for age-friendly and socially connected communities by focusing on older adults' social participation, intergenerational interaction, digital inclusion, and community wellbeing.

Using qualitative and design-led methods, including ethnographic fieldwork, stakeholder mapping, co-design workshops, and situated prototyping, the study aims to understand older adults' everyday community experiences and collaboratively explore service design opportunities with relevant stakeholders.

The expected contribution is a design framework for developing inclusive, participatory, and responsible digital community services that support ageing societies and intergenerational wellbeing.
