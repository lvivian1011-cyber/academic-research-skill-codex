# Theory Framework Map

## 1. Purpose of This File

This file maps the theoretical and conceptual frameworks that may support my PhD research direction.

The current research direction is:

**Intergenerational digital community services for ageing societies through participatory design, co-design, design anthropology, and responsible AI-supported service systems.**

The purpose of this map is to connect different theoretical areas and identify possible combinations for a PhD proposal, literature review, supervisor matching, and motivation letters.

## 2. Framework Area 1: Ageing and Community

### Key Concepts

- Ageing in place
- Age-friendly communities
- Age-friendly cities
- Active ageing
- Healthy ageing
- Social participation
- Community wellbeing
- Social inclusion
- Independence
- Autonomy
- Quality of life
- Community care

### Relevance to My PhD Direction

This framework helps position the research within ageing societies and older adults' everyday community life. It shifts the focus from institutional care or medicalised ageing toward community participation, local services, independence, and wellbeing.

### Possible Use in Proposal

This framework can support the argument that ageing is not only a healthcare issue, but also a community, service, social, and design issue.

### Possible Research Angle

How can community-based services support older adults' social participation, autonomy, and wellbeing while enabling them to remain connected to local community life?

## 3. Framework Area 2: Intergenerational Relations

### Key Concepts

- Intergenerational interaction
- Intergenerational learning
- Intergenerational care
- Intergenerational solidarity
- Mutual support
- Shared spaces
- Knowledge exchange
- Cross-generational participation
- Age-integrated community

### Relevance to My PhD Direction

This framework helps move beyond designing only for older adults. It positions ageing as a relational and community-based issue involving older adults, younger generations, families, neighbours, volunteers, local organisations, and public services.

### Possible Use in Proposal

This framework can support the argument that age-friendly communities should not separate older adults from other generations, but should create opportunities for meaningful intergenerational connection and mutual support.

### Possible Research Angle

How can design support shared spaces and services where different generations can interact, learn from each other, and contribute to community wellbeing?

## 4. Framework Area 3: Participatory Design and Co-design

### Key Concepts

- Participatory design
- Co-design
- User participation
- Stakeholder engagement
- Collaborative design
- Empowerment
- Democratic design
- Co-creation
- Participatory workshops
- Design facilitation

### Relevance to My PhD Direction

Participatory design and co-design provide methodological and ethical foundations for involving older adults and community stakeholders in the design process.

This is important because community services should not be designed only by experts, institutions, or technology providers. They need to reflect lived experiences, local practices, and stakeholder knowledge.

### Possible Use in Proposal

This framework can justify why older adults and community actors should be involved as active contributors rather than passive users.

### Possible Research Angle

How can participatory and co-design methods be adapted to include older adults and diverse community stakeholders in the development of digital community services?

## 5. Framework Area 4: Design Anthropology

### Key Concepts

- Everyday life
- Lived experience
- Ethnography
- Cultural practices
- Situated knowledge
- Material culture
- Social relations
- Local context
- Practice-based understanding
- Design intervention

### Relevance to My PhD Direction

Design anthropology helps understand ageing, care, community, and technology as situated in everyday life. It supports a deeper understanding of how older adults experience community services, social relationships, routines, places, and digital tools.

### Possible Use in Proposal

This framework can support an ethnographic and context-sensitive approach, avoiding abstract or technology-driven assumptions about older adults' needs.

### Possible Research Angle

How do older adults and community stakeholders experience intergenerational interaction, care, and digital services in everyday community life?

## 6. Framework Area 5: Service Design and Service Systems

### Key Concepts

- Service design
- Service ecosystem
- Product-service system
- Touchpoints
- User journey
- Stakeholder mapping
- Service blueprint
- Value co-creation
- Public service design
- Social service innovation
- Community service system

### Relevance to My PhD Direction

Service design provides a way to analyse and design community services as systems involving multiple actors, touchpoints, infrastructures, and interactions.

This is useful because intergenerational community services are not single products. They involve people, places, platforms, organisations, activities, and long-term relationships.

### Possible Use in Proposal

This framework can help structure the design outcome as a service system, not simply an app, product, or interface.

### Possible Research Angle

How can service design methods support the development of inclusive and sustainable intergenerational community service systems?

## 7. Framework Area 6: Inclusive Design and Digital Inclusion

### Key Concepts

- Inclusive design
- Universal design
- Accessibility
- Digital inclusion
- Digital exclusion
- Digital divide
- Digital literacy
- Social inclusion
- Usability
- Adaptability
- Diversity
- Equity

### Relevance to My PhD Direction

Inclusive design and digital inclusion are important because older adults may face barriers related to physical ability, digital literacy, social confidence, cognitive change, economic resources, or access to technology.

This framework helps ensure that digital community services do not exclude the very people they aim to support.

### Possible Use in Proposal

This framework can support the ethical and practical importance of designing accessible and inclusive community services.

### Possible Research Angle

How can digital community services be designed to reduce exclusion and support older adults with diverse abilities, experiences, and levels of digital confidence?

## 8. Framework Area 7: Responsible AI and Sociotechnical Systems

### Key Concepts

- Responsible AI
- Human-centred AI
- Ethical AI
- Trust
- Transparency
- Privacy
- Autonomy
- Data ethics
- Sociotechnical systems
- Algorithmic decision-making
- AI-supported services
- Technology acceptance

### Relevance to My PhD Direction

Responsible AI provides a possible future-facing dimension for the research. If AI is used in community services, it must be designed with attention to trust, transparency, autonomy, privacy, inclusion, and social consequences.

Sociotechnical systems theory helps frame technology not as a neutral tool, but as part of a wider system of people, institutions, practices, values, and infrastructures.

### Possible Use in Proposal

This framework can support the argument that AI-supported community services require design methods that are socially responsible, participatory, and context-sensitive.

### Possible Research Angle

How can responsible AI principles be translated into participatory service design practices for ageing-related community services?

## 10. Current Preferred Framework

The current preferred framework is:

**Ageing in place + intergenerational interaction + participatory service design + digital inclusion + community wellbeing**

This framework is strong because it connects my industrial design background with a broader PhD direction in design research, ageing, service systems, and community-based innovation.

## 11. Future Refinement Notes

The framework should be adjusted depending on the target supervisor.

For supervisors focused on service design:

- Emphasise service ecosystems, stakeholder mapping, and community service innovation.

For supervisors focused on design anthropology:

- Emphasise ethnography, everyday life, situated practices, and lived experience.

For supervisors focused on HCI or AI:

- Emphasise digital inclusion, responsible AI, trust, autonomy, and human-centred technology.

For supervisors focused on ageing:

- Emphasise ageing in place, social participation, wellbeing, care, and age-friendly communities.
