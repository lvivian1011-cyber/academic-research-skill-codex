# Research Identity

## 1. Current Research Identity

I am an industrial design educator and researcher with a background in product design, human factors, design psychology, and interdisciplinary design education. My current academic transition is from object-centred industrial design toward socially situated, community-based, and service-oriented design research.

My emerging PhD direction focuses on how design can support ageing societies through intergenerational digital community services, participatory design, co-design, design anthropology, and responsible AI-supported service systems.

Rather than treating older adults only as users of assistive technologies, I am interested in understanding them as active participants in community life, knowledge exchange, care relations, and local service innovation.

## 2. Academic and Professional Background

My background is rooted in Industrial Design and Art Design. I have teaching and research experience in areas such as:

- Industrial design
- Human factors and ergonomics
- Design psychology
- Smart product design
- User research
- Interdisciplinary design education
- Design for social and community contexts

In teaching, I have worked with students on product-service systems, smart products, user needs, design psychology, ergonomics, and interdisciplinary projects involving materials, automation, mechanical engineering, and enterprise-based design briefs.

This background gives me a strong foundation in human-centred design, user behaviour, applied design methods, and the translation of social or technological issues into design problems.

## 3. Research Transition

My current research development can be understood as a transition:

From:

- Product design
- Interface and interaction design
- Smart products
- Human factors and usability
- Design education

Toward:

- Service design
- Community-based design
- Ageing and intergenerational relations
- Participatory and co-design research
- Design anthropology
- Responsible digital and AI-supported services
- Socially engaged design research

This transition is important because my future PhD research should not only focus on designing objects or platforms, but also on understanding social relations, community practices, stakeholder participation, and long-term service ecosystems.

## 4. Core Research Interest

My core research interest is:

**Intergenerational digital community services for ageing societies.**

This direction explores how digital, service, and community-based design can support:

- Older adults' social participation
- Intergenerational interaction
- Community wellbeing
- Age-friendly environments
- Local knowledge exchange
- Mutual support between generations
- Responsible use of digital technologies and AI in community settings

## 5. Possible Research Focus

A possible PhD focus is to investigate how participatory and co-design methods can support the development of inclusive digital community services that connect older adults, younger generations, families, local organisations, and community stakeholders.

This may include studying:

- How older adults experience current community services
- How intergenerational interaction happens in everyday community life
- How digital platforms or AI-supported tools may support community participation
- How participatory design can involve older adults and stakeholders in service development
- How design can avoid technological exclusion, tokenistic participation, or top-down service models

## 6. Methodological Orientation

My methodological orientation is qualitative, participatory, and design-led. Relevant methods include:

- Ethnographic research
- Design anthropology
- Participatory design
- Co-design workshops
- Stakeholder mapping
- Service design methods
- Situated prototyping
- Scenario building
- User journey mapping
- Field observation
- Semi-structured interviews
- Community-based design interventions

I am particularly interested in combining design research with real-world community contexts, where design methods are used not only to produce solutions but also to generate knowledge about social relations, participation, ageing, care, and community wellbeing.

## 7. Theoretical Orientation

My possible theoretical foundations include:

- Ageing in place
- Age-friendly communities
- Intergenerational learning
- Social participation
- Community wellbeing
- Participatory design
- Co-design
- Design anthropology
- Service design
- Inclusive design
- Responsible AI

These theories can help frame my research as a design-led inquiry into how ageing, community life, technology, and service systems interact.

## 8. Long-term PhD Direction

My long-term PhD direction is to develop design knowledge, methods, and frameworks for creating inclusive, participatory, and responsible community services in ageing societies.

The research aims to contribute to:

- Design research on ageing and community wellbeing
- Participatory and co-design methods with older adults
- Service design for intergenerational communities
- Responsible digital and AI-supported community services
- Practical design strategies for age-friendly and intergenerational community innovation

## 9. Personal Positioning Statement

I position myself as an industrial design educator and emerging design researcher who is moving from product-centred design toward socially engaged, community-based, and service-oriented design research.

My PhD ambition is to explore how participatory design, co-design, and responsible digital technologies can support intergenerational community services, older adults' social participation, and inclusive community wellbeing in ageing societies.
