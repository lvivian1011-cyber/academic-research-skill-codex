# Academic Writing Style Guide

## General Style

Use clear, sincere, and academically grounded English.

The writing should be:

- precise;
- mature;
- research-oriented;
- exploratory;
- specific rather than generic;
- confident but not exaggerated.

Avoid overclaiming. Avoid sounding like the research proposal is already fixed.

## For Motivation Letters

- Use 3 to 4 paragraphs.
- Mention 1 or 2 specific works, themes, methods, or projects by the supervisor.
- Connect my previous design teaching and interdisciplinary experience to the supervisor's research.
- Keep the PhD idea open and exploratory.
- Avoid saying "I am certain this is the final topic".
- Avoid flattering language.
- Avoid generic phrases such as "your excellent research deeply attracts me".

## For Supervisor Emails

The email should usually include:

1. A brief self-introduction.
2. A concise explanation of my emerging PhD interest.
3. A specific connection to the supervisor's work.
4. A polite question about possible PhD supervision or research fit.

Keep the email concise, sincere, and specific.

## For Research Proposals

- Start from a social problem and research gap.
- Use design research language.
- Include possible methods but leave room for development.
- Connect ageing, community, service design, participatory methods, and responsible digital technology.
- Avoid presenting the proposed study as a finished project.

## Preferred Phrases

- I am particularly interested in...
- My previous teaching and project experience has led me to...
- I hope to further develop this direction through...
- This makes your work especially relevant to my emerging PhD interests...
- I am interested in exploring how...
- This possible direction may be developed through...

## Phrases to Avoid

- I am the perfect candidate for...
- I will definitely prove...
- My proposed research will completely solve...
- I am certain that my PhD topic is...
- Your research is extremely excellent and famous...
