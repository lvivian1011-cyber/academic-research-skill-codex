# Reading Workflow｜Literature Reading for PhD Application

Use this workflow every time a new paper is read.

The aim is to transform reading into application-ready material, not just produce a summary.

---

# 1. Before Reading

## 1.1 Identify why this paper is being read

Choose one or more reasons:

- [ ] It is written by a potential supervisor.
- [ ] It is central to ageing and community.
- [ ] It is central to intergenerational practice.
- [ ] It provides useful co-design / participatory methods.
- [ ] It provides useful design anthropology / ethnographic framing.
- [ ] It supports digital community services.
- [ ] It supports responsible AI / social acceptability.
- [ ] It supports service design / service ecosystem thinking.
- [ ] It may be useful for proposal literature review.
- [ ] It may be useful for motivation letter / personal statement.

## 1.2 Predict possible usefulness

Before reading fully, write:

> I expect this paper to be useful because...

# 3. Second Pass｜Structured Reading

Use [the canonical paper reading template](../templates/paper_reading_template.md) to produce one integrated note. `paper_note_template.md` remains a deep-reading companion, not a second output to duplicate.

Updated 2026-09-10: start with a copyable Reading Record; separate author claims and my inferences using claim IDs. Add proposal material by fixed module with a dated current English draft, and supervisor communication material including match, translation needed, email advice, a suggested opening paragraph and cautions. Include core keywords and 1–2 provisional research titles only when supported.

Focus on:

1. Research problem.
2. Theory / concepts.
3. Methodology.
4. Findings.
5. Limitations.
6. Research gap.
7. Relevance to my PhD.
8. Application in proposal, literature review, PS, and supervisor emails.

---

After analysing the paper, convert it into application material using sections 4–8 of the canonical template. Link each draft to its source claim ID; distinguish author meaning, my inference and proposed research. Do not repeat the same paragraph in several outputs.

## 4.1 Proposal conversion

Write 1–3 sentences for each section if relevant. Record the fixed module, current copyable English version, source, limits, status and date. Unsupported drafts must be marked as pending verification.

## 4.2 Supervisor-email conversion

Record the evidence-based match between the supervisor's work and my confirmed research background, the direction needing adaptation, and paper-specific cautions. Provide a brief suggested first paragraph (identity and purpose) separately from the literature hook; do not assume supervision availability or generate a full email unless requested.

Write a possible email hook:

> I was particularly interested in your work on [...], especially how it [...]. This connects with my emerging PhD interest in [...].

## 4.3 Personal statement conversion

Write a possible PS sentence:

> My interest in [...] has been shaped by literature that shows [...], which resonates with my background in Industrial Design and my aim to explore [...].

# 7. Recommended Output Format for ChatGPT

When asking ChatGPT to read a paper, use this instruction:

```text
Please read this paper using my PhD literature-reading workflow. Do not only summarize it. Output a structured note including: research problem, theoretical framework, methodology, findings, limitations, relevance to my PhD direction, relevance score, and how I can use it in my proposal, literature review, supervisor email, and personal statement.
```

For supervisor papers, use this instruction:

```text
Please read this paper as a potential supervisor-matching paper. Focus on the author's research agenda, methods, theoretical position, fit with my PhD direction, and whether it gives me a strong reason to contact this supervisor.
```

For proposal-building papers, use this instruction:

```text
Please read this paper for proposal development. Extract the research gap, theoretical framing, methodology, and 3–5 sentences I could adapt into my research proposal.
```
